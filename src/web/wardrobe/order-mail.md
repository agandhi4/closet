# Order email import, "From your orders" (#25)

Part of the Wardrobe (`AGENTS.md` in this directory). Plan: `docs/plans/2026-09-28-order-email-import.md`. Owner setup: `docs/operations.md`, Order email import (the token: Settings › Privacy & Security › Manage API tokens, Email and Read-only access ticked; [Fastmail's help](https://www.fastmail.help/hc/en-us/articles/5254602856719-API-tokens)).

The owner forwards order emails to a dedicated Fastmail account. Closet polls it through JMAP with a read-only token and lists the products it finds for review. **Opt-in**: without `ORDER_MAIL_JMAP_TOKEN`, `createApp()` builds nothing, so there is no timer or menu entry and the review list's routes answer 404. With the token, `ORDER_MAIL_SENDERS` and `ORDER_MAIL_OWNER` are required, and `loadConfig` refuses the boot without them.

## Layout

```
  order-mail/          jmap.ts (the JMAP client: session, inbox ids since a watermark, envelopes, bodies),
                       trust.ts (judgeSender, pure), links.ts (productLinkCandidates, normalizedLink, pure),

```

## The poll

`scheduleMinutely({ everyMinutes: ORDER_MAIL_POLL_MINUTES })` runs `pollOrderMailWhole`, wrapped in `metrics.timeJob('order_mail')`: a failure is the job's `failure` outcome and a Bugsink event. Each run:

1. Opens the JMAP session.
2. Reads `ORDER_MAIL_OWNER`'s account, the watermark (the oldest failing email's `received_at` in the session's account, else the newest) and the failing emails in one statement (`pollStart`, #173; the watermark needs the session's account id, so the owner is looked up after the session). No account is `OrderMailOwnerError`, before any email is read or recorded.
3. Queries the inbox from the watermark (inclusive), parks a failing email at the watermark that the answer lacks (it left the inbox: `gone`), drops the settled ids (decided or parked) and takes at most `MAX_EMAILS_PER_POLL` (5).
4. Fetches the envelopes (From, `Authentication-Results`) and judges each sender.
5. Fetches the body of a trusted email only.
6. Judges its links before any fetch (`emailLinks`, below: product paths, then at most 2 trackers, 12 in all; the rest counted as `skipped` in the email's log line), fetches each through the link import's `readProductPage` (the user-URL fetcher, GET, no cookie or credential) with `isProductLink` as its `mayFollow`, and keeps product pages that ended on a product link.
7. Records the email and its items atomically (`recordOrderEmail`): with products, one transaction of four statements (the email and its items in one CTE statement, then its count and outcome); without, one insert.

Statements per run: 2 when nothing is new (the poll every few minutes), plus per new email 1 without products or 4 with them, 1 more per failed read, and 1 to park emails gone from the inbox (`order-mail.spec.ts` pins them).

**Each email has its own outcome** (#693; `order_email.status`, `ORDER_EMAIL_STATUSES`): `decided`, `failed` or `parked`. A read that throws is caught per email and recorded by `recordOrderEmailFailure` (one upsert, on the pool, outside the transaction the email's own write rolled back), and the poll goes on. Whose failure it was is `JmapError.scope`: three scopes (`OrderEmailFailureScope`, #932; the one rule is `failureScope`). `email`: a 4xx for that id, or a database refusal of the email's values: spends one `attempts`. `message`: that id's own body fetch answering a 5xx or a wrong shape: fails the run and counts `mailbox_failures` on its row, and `ORDER_MAIL_MAILBOX_STRIKES` (3) in a row spend one attempt. `outage`: everything else (the session, the query, the envelopes, the transport, 401/403/408/429, a JMAP method error, an answer that is not JSON, the database out of reach, its pool full, a timeout, a serialization failure, a deadlock): fails the run and writes no row, so it spends neither. A `mailbox_failures` value left by the old rule is not reset by the next strike, which `recordOrderEmailFailure` increments from it: an email left at 2 spends an attempt on its first `message` strike; an `email` failure or a decided outcome moves it. A run stopped that way after it failed an email's first attempt throws one `PartialFailure` with the stopping error as its `cause`, so that email is still captured once. `ORDER_MAIL_ATTEMPTS` (3, one per poll, never timed) park it: never read again, and the watermark passes it. Every outcome reaches a `failed` row through the same guarded upsert (`where status = 'failed'`), so a retry that succeeds stops pinning the watermark and a decided or parked row is never touched again. `error_kind` keeps a SQLSTATE, an error's name or `gone`, never a message. The job is a `PartialFailure` on every poll while any email is `failed`; it is captured only by the run whose read took an email's attempts from 0 to 1 (`PartialFailure.reported`), so one email is one Bugsink event. An email a run did not finish otherwise has no row and is read again next time. `order_item`'s `(owner_id, product_url)` uniqueness makes a re-read, or a shipping email naming the same product, list nothing twice. Logs use logger `OrderMail`.

## The review list

`GET /wardrobe/orders`, and `POST /wardrobe/orders/:id/add` and `/dismiss`, both native `PostForm`s (disabled offline). They answer only the `ORDER_MAIL_OWNER` account; anyone else, and everyone with the order mail off, gets a 404. They are registered even when off: unregistered, `/wardrobe/orders` fell to `/wardrobe/:id`, whose id check answered 400. The Wardrobe's ⋯ menu links the list when `ViewContext.orderReview` is set.

"Add to closet" runs `importLink` on the stored product link and renders the ordinary new-garment form for the closet. The form carries the photo choices, the order's day as `dateAquired`, the stored price when the page has none in dollars, and `orderItem` (hidden, with a notice). `POST /wardrobe` resolves `orderItem` in `postedDestination`: the requester's own pending item in their own wardrobe, else a 404 before anything is stored. It then marks the item `added` in the garment's own transaction (`WithGarment`, as a plan candidate link). A page that cannot be fetched now opens the form with the stored details and a notice. Fetching counts against a `LINK_IMPORT_LIMIT` checker of its own.

Tests: `test/integration/order-mail.spec.ts` (the poll against the JMAP stub and a local shop, the review list, the off state), `test/order-review.spec.ts (retired 2026-10-13)` (the list at 390 px, seeded rows, dismiss, the fallback form).

## Gotchas

- **The token reaches only the session URL's host.** The JMAP client passes `hosts` and `authorization` to its own outbound fetcher. A request with a credential or a body follows no redirect, and the fetcher throws, as a programming error, when either comes without `hosts` (Request security, Outbound fetches). Every error is a `JmapError` naming the step and the fetcher's reason, never a header. `scrubEvent` also filters `fm?N-…` token shapes and the `ORDER_MAIL_JMAP_TOKEN` key.
- **Only the topmost `Authentication-Results` counts, and it must be Fastmail's** (its inbound MX names itself `*-mx-NN.messagingengine.com`). A sender can add passing headers of their own below it. `judgeSender` also needs the single From address to be in `ORDER_MAIL_SENDERS`, and DKIM, SPF or DMARC to pass for a domain aligned with it. An untrusted email's body is never fetched.
- **A link is fetched only when its shape says product, judged before any fetch** (#698, `links.ts`). A fetch is a GET the retailer sees as a click, and a "Cancel this order", "Confirm your email" or single-use "View your order" link acts on one, so the rule is an allowlist, never a deny list (which fetches every action word nobody listed). `judgeLink` unwraps a redirector carrying its target in the query (Google's `?q=`, Outlook, `?url=`) and judges the target: it must be https and either a product path (`isProductLink`: `/p/`, `/dp/`, `/product(s)/`, `/item(s)/` segment, or a last segment naming the SKU as a page (`shirt-p01234567.html`), never a bare numeric id, with no denied host label, path segment, query key or whole query value, and no credential or action parameter: `token`, `sig`, `code`, `action`, ...; `?code=SAVE10` is refused too, fail closed) or an opaque tracker of a known shape (`/ls/click`, `/wf/click`, `/CL0/`, a `click.` or `links.` host) with at least one anchor whose text or `alt` is not empty (a text part's link has none) and none of whose anchors' text or image `alt` names an action (cancel, confirm, view order, sign in, manage, unsubscribe...). Every check reads one canonical form of the address (`canonicalUrl`: no fragment, the path's percent-escaped unreserved characters (letters, digits, - . _ ~) decoded, #949), so `/%63ancel` is `/cancel` to all of them. Words match whole segments and tokens: `/products/confirm-hoodie` passes, `/products/x?action=cancel` does not; campaign tags (`utm_medium=order`) are labels and skipped. Every redirect must pass `isProductLink` before it is resolved (the fetcher's `mayFollow`), so a tracker bouncing to `/account/cancel` is abandoned at that hop; a meta-refresh or script redirect is never followed, and a fetch that did not end on a product link lists nothing. The tracker hop is the one click a forward costs. A shop whose product addresses have none of these shapes lists nothing (a manual "Add from a link"); trusting a host because an earlier link yielded a product was declined: it reopens that host to the deny list alone. List-Unsubscribe headers are never read.
- **An email's HTML is scanned, never rendered.** Its links come from the link import's linear scanner (`anchors`, at most 200, each with its text). The review page shows only what the product page said (name, brand, price) and the store's host.
- **A fetched link keeps its query as written, since a tracker's may be signed.** Tracking parameters come off only the product page's final address (`normalizedLink`), which is what is stored and compared.
- **An order item is never a garment or a wishlist row until it is added.** The wishlist is shared with VIEW grantees (a gift would show), and it feeds "Goes with my closet", plan candidates and the MCP tools. A new reader of `order_item` names its owner. A new move of `state` goes through `decideOrderItem` (pending only, once).
