# Link import

Part of the Wardrobe (`src/web/wardrobe/AGENTS.md`).

- **Link import** (#6, `link-import/`: `routes.tsx`, `import.ts` the fetch and the pending photo, `prefill.ts` the form values, `link-page.tsx`, `photo-choice.tsx`, and the extractor): `extractProduct(html, pageUrl)` (`extract.ts`, pure) turns a product page into form prefills: schema.org Product JSON-LD (also inside `@graph`, arrays, a WebPage's `mainEntity`, a ProductGroup's variants), then Open Graph and `product:` tags, then `<title>`, each field from the first source that has it; price as a decimal string with its ISO currency; up to 8 image candidates, absolute (against `<base href>`), http(s) only, each once. `guess.ts` maps words to the form's values: colours onto `GARMENT_COLORS` ("navy" is blue), category and type from the name (the phrase that ends last wins: "shirt dress" is a dress), materials, a stated weight ("240 gsm", "14 oz") to `fabricWeight`, and "heavyweight"/"lightweight" without a number to the type's weight-step warmth. `html.ts` is a linear tag scanner, not a parser: the page is a stranger's, so nothing may throw, backtrack or recurse on it (`extract.spec.ts` feeds it broken and hostile pages).
  - **The flow**: "Add from a link" on the new garment form (or Android's share sheet, by way of the service worker's redirect) opens the link page; it fetches nothing until Fetch (a GET never writes). `importLink` (`import.ts`) fetches with `accept: ['html', 'image']`: an image is stored as the **pending photo**; a page goes through `extractProduct`, then up to `MAX_PHOTO_CHOICES` (6) of its image candidates go through `previewChoices`, each turned into a small WebP preview in memory (`Photos.preview`), and the first readable one (in the page's order) stored as the pending photo. `importedForm` (`prefill.ts`, pure) makes the form values: the category's presets fill what the page did not say (as `withPresets`), a stated weight shows in gsm, the price only when in dollars or without a currency (else a notice names it), the product link is the link as given. The answer is the ordinary new-garment form (posting to `POST /wardrobe`) with notices, the pending photo (hidden `linkPhoto` plus its thumb) and, for two or more readable candidates, the choices as buttons with `data:` previews. A tap posts `url` and the current `linkPhoto` (`hx-params`) to the photo route, which stores the pick and discards the photo it replaces; the taps share one queue on the choice group (see Gotchas). The link field is `type="text"` with `inputmode="url"`: a share's "Title https://…" is valid input, and `type="url"` had the browser refuse it before the post. `extractProduct` also says its `confidence` (`product`, `page`, `title-only`; a link redirected up its own path to the shop's home is `title-only`, `leftProductPath`): `title-only` prefills only the link, warns (`TITLE_ONLY`, or `NOTHING_FOUND` for an empty page) and fetches no photo; the MCP tools and the API answer carry it (#675, `docs/decisions.md` 2026-11-14). A page with nothing extractable opens the form with the link kept and a message. A page past 2 MB is read up to it (the fetcher cuts it), so a giant page with its JSON-LD first still imports; a shop that refuses automated fetches is `blocked-by-site` (`linkImport.BLOCKED_BY_SITE`; the MCP tools' errors lead with the reason, so Muse takes another retailer), never a retry: #347 found Shopify product pages 429 HTTP/1.1 clients and serve HTTP/2, which the fetcher now speaks (#496b), with the same honest User-Agent; a 429 over h2, Cloudflare's and AWS WAF's challenges stay refused (`docs/decisions.md` 2026-11-12, 2026-11-13 #496b). `link-import.spec.ts` serves fixture pages from 127.0.0.2 (`test/integration/link-sites.ts`).
  - **A pending photo** is bytes without a `file` row plus a `pending_photo` row naming the user who fetched it (a MANAGE grantee's import is theirs until saved), recorded through `keepPendingPhoto` with the per-user cap (see Images), as the add sheet's upload is. The save claims it: `createGarmentWithPendingPhoto` (`writes.ts`) takes the pending row (the requester's only), inserts the `file` row (queued for its cutout, owned by the wardrobe's owner) and the garment in one transaction, and refuses (the form again, "no longer available") a name with no pending row of the requester's, no bytes, or a `file` row already. `discardPendingPhoto` deletes only the requester's own pending photo. Nothing reaches `garment` or `file` before the save; reconciliation removes a pending photo a day old with its row, outside the deletion guard.
  - **Memory** (#120): `previewChoices` works on `CHOICE_CONCURRENCY` (2) candidates at a time and keeps only the first readable one's bytes past its preview, so an import holds at most three fetched images of up to 15 MB and two decodes, whatever the page names. Before decoding, each candidate's pixels are read from its header (`Photos.inputPixels`) and taken from `CHOICE_PIXEL_BUDGET`, which is `MAX_INPUT_PIXELS` (one upload's worth, 64 MP) for all the choices together; one past what is left is skipped with a warning, never failing the import. Storing the pick decodes it once more afterwards. Six near-limit photos used to be decoded at once (~1.5 GB of pixels). `LINK_IMPORT_LIMIT` bounds one user's rate; concurrent users multiply it.
  - **Saved at once, without the form** (`save.ts`, `saveGarmentFromLink`): MCP's `add_garment_from_link` and `suggest_garment`, and the API's link import (#192c). `importLink`, `importedForm`, the caller's overrides (a new category or type brings its presets), `readGarmentForm`, then the claim (`createGarmentWithPendingPhoto`); the first readable photo, no choice. A refused form discards the pending photo and is returned for the caller to word (MCP a 400 naming form fields, the API `body/<pointer>` errors); a refused fetch is a 400 or 422 leading with its reason (the page's 502 would be a detail-free 5xx on the API). A new surface that saves from a link calls it, never re-composes the steps.
  - **The order mail reads through it** (#25, `../order-mail.md`): the poll reads each link of a forwarded order email with `readProductPage` (`import.ts`: fetch as HTML, `extractProduct`, nothing stored), and the review list's "Add to closet" runs `importLink` on the kept link. `html.ts` also collects `<a href>` values (`links`, at most `MAX_LINKS`) for it; the poll passes `readProductPage` its `mayFollow`, `importLink` none.
  - **One rate limit for every fetching route.** The import, the photo choice and the API's `POST /api/v1/garments/link-imports` count on one checker, `linkImportLimit = app.createRateLimit(LINK_IMPORT_LIMIT)`, built once in `webPlugin` and passed in the options (`LinkImportLimit`): ten fetching requests a minute per user between them, keyed by the session's user or the device token's. Past it the import (a native post) throws `tooManyAttempts` (the 429 page, as a route-config limit answers) and so does the photo choice (an hx-post: the error handler's 429 trigger, a toast, and the slot keeps the photo it had; its fetch refusals too, with their `REFUSALS` status, #642). The MCP tool's `MCP_LINK_IMPORT_LIMIT` is a separate budget by design (MCP server).

## Gotchas

- **The form's `linkPhoto` is a name the client sent: the `pending_photo` row is the capability, not the name.** Claiming (`createGarmentWithPendingPhoto`) and discarding (`discardPendingPhoto`) both require a pending row for that name **and the requesting user** (and, for a batch's draft, the wardrobe it was uploaded for: `PendingScope`, #200), so knowing another user's pending name (or a saved photo's) gets nothing. `commitWithPhoto` deletes the new bytes when its transaction fails, which is right only for bytes the same request wrote: the claim never deletes bytes, and the discard deletes them only after taking its own pending row. Both also take `lockOwnersAndPhotoName` (`src/web/files/queries.ts`: the locks of every account whose rows they write, the uploader's and for a save the wardrobe owner's, in id order, then a transaction advisory lock on the name, in one statement) and check that no `file` row exists; the pending row's delete orders a save, a discard, an eviction and reconciliation, so exactly one of them wins.
- **Link import's photo choices are never stored.** Only the pick is: the candidates are previews inline as `data:` URLs (the CSP's `img-src` allows `data:` and no other origin, which is also why remote images cannot simply be shown), and the photo a pick replaces is discarded at once. With the per-user cap of 10 pending photos, one user can never hold more than 10 stored imports.
- **`createRateLimit` and a route's `config.rateLimit` never share counters.** Each route config and each `createRateLimit` call gets its own store (`LocalStore.child` returns a new one), so the same options on two routes is two budgets. A limit two routes share is one `createRateLimit` checker called from both handlers (`linkImportLimit`); `link-import.spec.ts` interleaves imports and choices and refuses the combined eleventh.
- **Photo choices are serialized on the group, not the form** (`hx-sync="#link-photo-choices:queue last"`). Two picks in flight both posted the same `linkPhoto`: only one could discard it, and the other's new photo lost its only reference (an orphan until reconciliation). A queued pick reads the form when it is sent, after the slot has taken the previous answer. Not `closest form`: that is the garment form's autosave queue, where a property change would drop a waiting pick. The group is never swapped (answers go to `#link-photo`), or its queue would die with it.
- **JSON-LD is found by the type's essence** (`application/ld+json; charset=utf-8` counts), and **an empty Product node is passed over** (`{"@type": "Product", "@id": "#product"}` before the populated block), as are `faux-`, `vegan-` and `PU-leather`, which are synthetic, never leather.
- **A shared link reaches the link page by GET, which fetches nothing.** Android's share is a POST navigation to `/inspiration/share` that the service worker answers itself, never sending it on (one manifest has one target, which also takes photos: Inspiration's doc): a 303 to `/wardrobe/new/from-link?url=<link>` (or `?text=` with no link in it; `linkIn` lives in `link-text.ts`, which imports nothing, for the worker's bundle); without `PWA_ENABLED` the manifest's target is that GET itself (`title`, `text`, `url`; the link arrives in either of the last two, `linkIn` takes the first http(s) link). Fetching stores a pending photo, and a GET never writes. The person taps Fetch, or "Save as inspiration" (own closet only).
- **What the extractor hands on is what the columns hold** (#693): every text field goes through `plainText` (`html.ts`: entities decoded, then control characters, NUL included, removed) and `parsePrice` admits only what `numeric(10,2)` stores after Postgres rounds it to cents (not 0.00, not past 99999999.99), so neither the poll's `order_item` nor a garment can be refused for a page's values. A new extracted field goes through them.
